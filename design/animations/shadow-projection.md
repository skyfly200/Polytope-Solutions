# Shadow Projection

**Movement:** Shadow Projection
**Site:** Polytope Solutions — "Gain Higher Perspectives"

## Philosophy

Every shadow is the trace of something living one dimension above the one we can see. The figure in this piece is never drawn directly. It is a four-dimensional lattice of points sitting on the surface of a hypersphere, and what reaches the screen is only its projection, the way a cube becomes a hexagon on a wall. The algorithm's whole purpose is to make that hidden structure legible through its shadow, and the craft lives in how faithfully the projection carries the geometry without ever showing the geometry itself. This should read as though it took countless hours to develop, was refined with patient care, and comes from someone at the absolute top of computational geometry. Every ratio of the rotation and every projection distance was tuned through painstaking iteration.

The structure is computed once per seed. Points are drawn from a seeded Gaussian distribution and normalized onto the unit 3-sphere, which yields a uniform scatter across the 4D surface. Each point is then linked to its nearest neighbors measured in four dimensions, not on the screen. Because the motion below is made of pure rotations, which preserve distance, this graph is invariant for the life of the seed. The lines are not drawn, they are inherited. Only the way the structure casts its shadow changes, so the figure keeps its identity while constantly rearranging itself.

Motion comes from three independent rotations, one in each pair of orthogonal planes that a 4D space allows. Each seed sets its own rates and phase offsets, with ratios chosen to be incommensurate so the motion never closes into a simple loop. A slow viewer drift adds a second layer of perspective: at rest the camera eases in a gentle sway, and when the cursor is over the canvas it turns the viewing angle toward the pointer. The whole piece is a single continuous process, not a sequence of frames, and the same seed always unfolds the same way for anyone who watches it.

Projection is two-stage. The 4D points are first perspective-projected into three dimensions, then into the flat canvas, with the camera distances kept deliberately modest so the foreshortening stays elegant rather than chaotic. The fourth coordinate, which the eye cannot see directly, is translated into color and weight: points and links that sit nearer the viewer in the hidden dimension glow amber, and those receding into it fade toward violet. Long links, which span a large distance in four dimensions, are dimmed so the eye reads the structure's near geometry first. Trails are produced by fading the previous frame toward black rather than clearing it, which turns the process into a memory of its own earlier shadows.

Restraint is the discipline. The canvas is black, the strokes are hairline, and the palette is the site's own violet and amber, used sparingly enough that the geometry does the talking. Parameters are the viewer's lens and nothing more: point density, link density, the depth of the projection, the speed of the turning, and the length of the trails. Seeds are the variations, each one a different hidden figure and a different cadence of turning, so exploring them is like turning a single object over in the hand. The final algorithm should feel as though it were built by a master-level generative artist who discarded a thousand near-misses to keep only the one that reveals the most with the least.

**Conceptual seed (for the team, not for the UI):** The piece borrows the logic of Edwin Abbott's *Flatland*, in which a visitor from a higher dimension is perceived only through the cross-sections and shadows it casts. The site's own promise — seeing a problem from a new angle — is the same idea applied to consulting. The viewer never sees the object, only what it projects, and that is the service being offered.
